Three ways to open the app

One design language for the four homes: school admin, teacher, parent and the Brillanda team. Same data, same tokens, three different ideas of what the first screen should be. Click things: marks, rings, stages, buttons, and try Ctrl K.

My pick: C as the frame for every role, B's constellation as the admin's drill-down, and A's rules for type, spacing and motion everywhere.

What the research said
  • Calm beats complete. The most-admired 2026 products (Linear, Vercel) remove everything that doesn't move you to the next decision. Every draft opens with one sentence and one number.
  • Progressive disclosure. Show the next decision, reveal detail on demand: the side sheet in B and C, the rows in A.
  • The key number goes top-left, and it's different per role: subjects in (admin), classes finished (teacher), position and average (parent), trial requests (team).
  • Role-adaptive homes, one shell. Same nav pattern and components; different defaults per role (HubSpot, Asana).
  • Command palette. Ctrl/⌘ K to jump to a class, find a student or run an action (Linear, Raycast, Slack).
  • Design empty states up front. The "Day one" toggle shows each home before any score exists.
  • Warmth in B2B. Small, earned moments: the morphing "Mark complete" button, the published toast. No confetti, glows or bouncy easing; that follows the motion brief in your PDF.
  • Where local products fall short. SAFSMS and Edves open on a wall of widgets (finance, events, attendance). Brillanda's edge is focus on the results cycle, and honesty about what's still to come.
